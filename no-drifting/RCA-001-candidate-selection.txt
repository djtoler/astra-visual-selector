RCA-001  WHY THE SYSTEM DOES NOT LAND THE RIGHT CANDIDATE ON THE RIGHT BEAT
Written 2026-09-20. Author: Claude. All numbers measured, not estimated; the command
that produced each is reproducible from the repo.

================================================================================
1. OBJECTIVE
================================================================================
Land the right media candidate on the right beat.

Unpacked, so it can be tested rather than admired:

  Given a passage of narration, produce for each beat a small set of treatments such
  that at least one of them communicates what the beat is saying, and none of them
  communicates something the beat does not say.

Two halves, and we have been treating only the first as the problem:
  RECALL     the right treatment is somewhere in the set
  PRECISION  the wrong treatments are not in the set

At production scale the target is ~20 videos/day across niches, so whatever we build
has to run without a human in the loop on the common case.

================================================================================
2. CURRENT APPROACH, AS BUILT
================================================================================
Six stages. Marked MODEL or DETERMINISTIC per the design principle in CLAUDE.md.

STAGE 1  Narration -> beats                      MODEL      pipeline/PROMPT-beats.md
  Per beat: quote, job (one of 20), entity_count, entity_kind, takeaway,
  must_be_true[], must_be_perceptible[], would_be_a_lie[], unstated[].
  Currently 40 beats over 435s of 823.5s of narration.

STAGE 2  Template pool                           DETERMINISTIC  candidates.py load()
  approved-list.json, enriched from description-inventory and catalog.
  421 records. Per record: description, useWhen, avoid, axes (declared capacity),
  clip path, and the source of every field.

STAGE 3  Job binding                             MODEL, offline, once   bind.py
  Every record judged against all 20 jobs. 22 calls, $3.74, 0 truncated.
  642 bindings, each carrying promptSha, model, runId, verdict, mechanism, evidence.
  validate_bindings.py refuses anything without all six.

STAGE 4  Slate                                   DETERMINISTIC  diversify()
  INPUT: the beat's job. Nothing else.
  Look up job -> 7 to 249 bound rows. Group by template family, order families
  rarest-first, take one row per family preferring the rarest mechanism cluster,
  cap at 12.

STAGE 5  Human review                            pipeline/ui2
  User picks up to 6 per beat, or flags the beat as raw b-roll.

STAGE 6  Grammar                                 primaries.json (currently empty)
  Production would use a job's primary treatment.

================================================================================
3. WHAT ACTUALLY REACHES THE CHOOSER
================================================================================
This is the finding the rest of the analysis rests on.

  BEAT FIELD            PRODUCED BY   USED BY SELECTION?
  job                   stage 1       YES  - the only key
  entity_count          stage 1       no
  entity_kind           stage 1       no
  takeaway              stage 1       no   - passed to the UI for the human to read
  must_be_true[]        stage 1       no   - same
  must_be_perceptible[] stage 1       no   - same
  would_be_a_lie[]      stage 1       no   - same
  unstated[]            stage 1       no   - same

  RECORD FIELD          USED BY SELECTION?
  description           stage 3 only, judged against the JOB, never against the beat
  useWhen / avoid       stage 3 only
  axes (capacity)       NOT USED. shape() is never called from shotlist.py.
  mechanism             tiebreak only: decides which scene a family contributes

Verified by grep: shotlist.py calls C.load(), C.diversify(), C.capacity_band().
It never calls C.shape() or C.bounds(). The +/-33% tolerance rule the user specified
personally, implemented and documented in CLAUDE.md, is not in the selection path.

MEASURED CONSEQUENCE
  40 beats produce 20 distinct slates. Half of all beats see a list identical to
  another beat's, because the slate is a pure function of the job label.

  Example. Both of these are job=narrate_an_event and therefore see the same 12:
    02a  "Curren$y was on the 2009 XXL Freshman cover"          entity_count 1
    28   (the other narrate_an_event beat)
  133 treatments are bound to that job. All 133 are candidates for a beat whose
  requirement is one portrait and a caption.

WHAT THE BEAT ACTUALLY KNOWS AND WE THROW AWAY
  beat 09, parallel_instances, entity_count 4:
    must_be_perceptible: "The four percentages must be distinguishable as separate,
    escalating values (50, 52, 71, 79), each anchored to its own artist."
  beat 11a, one_vs_aggregate, entity_count 10:
    must_be_perceptible: "Both shares must read as small slivers of a whole, not as
    dominant portions."
  beat 03, explain_the_encoding, entity_count 93:
    must_be_perceptible: "a visible low-to-high ordering of dots by height, readable
    starting from the bottom."

  These are precise, checkable visual specifications. The extraction stage is doing
  good work. Selection discards all of it and uses one word.

================================================================================
4. PROBLEMS OBSERVED, WITH EVIDENCE
================================================================================
P1  Options are wrong or dominated.
    User verdict, repeatedly. Most recently 7 scenes of one pack on beat 02.02a.

P2  Half the beats get an identical slate.  40 beats -> 20 slates.

P3  The beat's capacity requirement is ignored.
    Applying shape() with each beat's entity_count across all 40 beats:
      exact    309  23%
      within   150  11%
      unknown  659  49%
      outside  219  16%   <- would be removed by a filter we built and never wired in
    Beat 03 (n=93): all 4 candidates are outside. The slate currently shows 1 of them
    with no indication that nothing in the library can carry 93 entities.

P4  Two of the three slate rules do not do what the code says.
    Measured by disabling each axis and diffing every slate:
      remove the cluster cap only ......  0 rows change
      remove clustering entirely ....... 12 rows change, 4 of 20 jobs
      remove the family cap ............  0 rows change
    FAM_MAX is declared and never read. CLUSTER_MAX is read and never fires. The two
    tests asserting them pass vacuously, by observing the loop rather than the constant.

P5  Defect rate. Every defect this project has had was found by the user reading a UI.
    Until today there were zero tests against 637 lines of deterministic code.

P6  Library gaps, measured earlier and unchanged.
    45 of 420 records have never been rendered to video.
    49% of records declare no capacity at all, which caps what any capacity filter
    can discriminate.
    Roughly 43 of 468 records can carry a quantity, in a corpus meant to serve
    data-driven documentary narration.

================================================================================
5. FIVE WHYS - CHAIN A: WHY THE CANDIDATES ARE WRONG FOR THE BEAT
================================================================================
W1  Why are the options wrong for this beat?
    Because the slate is drawn from the job's bound set using diversity alone.
    Nothing in the selection step consults what this beat requires.

W2  Why does nothing consult the beat's requirements?
    Because the only beat field that reaches selection is `job`. entity_count,
    entity_kind, must_be_perceptible, must_be_true and would_be_a_lie are carried
    through shotlist.py into the output dict and read only by the human in the UI.

W3  Why is only `job` used?
    Because stage 3 binds treatments to jobs offline, and stage 4 was designed as a
    lookup. A lookup takes one key. The job became that key.

W4  Why was a single key acceptable at design time?
    Because the inversion away from per-beat catalog search was motivated by cost and
    determinism: at ~500 beats/day a model call per beat looked expensive, slow and
    unrepeatable. Collapsing the beat to a job made selection free.

W5  Why did collapsing the beat to a job seem safe?
    Because the members of a job's bound set were assumed roughly interchangeable -
    "any treatment that can narrate an event will do for a beat that narrates an
    event." They are not interchangeable. narrate_an_event binds 133 records and
    enumerate binds 249, and inside those sets the members differ on precisely the
    dimensions the beat specifies: how many things they can hold, whether a numeric
    value is readable, whether they imply ranking.

W6  Why was that assumption never caught?
    Because no test asserted "the slate satisfies what the beat stated." No component
    had a written definition of correct, so nothing could check one. 40 beats
    collapsing to 20 slates produced no warning from anything.

W7  Why did no component have a written definition of correct?
    Because requirements lived in prose - in CLAUDE.md, in a message, in my reading of
    a situation - and prose cannot fail. Only an executable assertion can fail.

================================================================================
6. FIVE WHYS - CHAIN B: WHY THE DEFECT RATE IS SO HIGH
================================================================================
W1  Why do so many defects reach the user?
    Because nothing checked the code before the user saw it.

W2  Why did nothing check it?
    No tests existed until today.

W3  Why no tests?
    Each component was specified in prose and reviewed by eye in a UI. The UI was the
    test harness, and the user was the assertion.

W4  Why was prose the specification?
    Because features were justified by narrative - "cluster the mechanisms to
    diversify the slate" - rather than by a problem statement with a measurable
    before and after.

W5  Why narrative justification?
    Because I optimised for producing something visible each turn instead of something
    verified. A feature that sounds right ships faster than a feature that is shown to
    be right, and the difference only surfaces when the user finds the defect.

W6  Why did that survive so long?
    Same as A-W7. With no definition of correct, "shipped" and "working" were
    indistinguishable, so there was no signal to correct against.

================================================================================
7. ROOT CAUSE
================================================================================
The two chains converge on one thing.

  ROOT CAUSE
  Requirements were never written in a form that anything could check.

  In chain A the unwritten requirement is the BEAT'S. The extraction stage produces a
  real specification - capacity, what must be perceptible, what would be a lie - and
  the pipeline compresses it to a single label before selection, so the specification
  reaches a human's eyes and nothing else. The system is a job-to-treatment lookup
  presented as a beat-to-treatment matcher.

  In chain B the unwritten requirement is the COMPONENT'S. Every rule lived in prose,
  so no rule could fail, so FAM_MAX could be declared and never read, and the test
  written to guard it could pass without guarding anything.

  These are the same failure at two levels: a specification that exists, is correct,
  and is not connected to anything that enforces it.

The user's new rule - problem, explanation, test - is the exact corrective for chain B.
Chain A needs its own corrective: the beat's specification must become an input to
selection, not a caption on the review card.

================================================================================
8. PROPOSED SOLUTIONS
================================================================================
Ordered by cost. Each states the problem it solves, why that is the real problem, and
the test that validates it - per the no-drifting rule.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S1  WIRE THE BEAT'S CAPACITY REQUIREMENT INTO SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM     The beat states entity_count. shape() implements the +/-33% tolerance
            rule. They have never been connected.
WHY REAL    A treatment that cannot hold the number of things the beat names cannot
            communicate the beat, whatever else is true of it. This is the cheapest
            available precision, and it is already built.
CHANGE      shotlist.py filters the bound set with C.shape(rows, beat.entity_count)
            before diversify. Keep exact, within, unknown. Drop outside. Order
            exact > within > unknown. If the filter would empty the slate, keep the
            unfiltered set and flag the beat - per the standing rule that slot counts
            never disqualify, they flag.
TEST        - no slate contains a record whose declared capacity is outside the beat's
              tolerance band, unless the beat is flagged capacity_impossible
            - beat 03 (n=93, all 4 outside) is flagged, not silently shown
            - a beat whose candidates are all unknown-capacity is unchanged
EXPECTED    Removes 16% of candidates across the corpus. Beat 07 goes 249 -> 187.
HONEST LIMIT
            16% is small, because 49% of records declare no capacity. This is
            necessary and nowhere near sufficient. On its own it will not fix P1.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S2  MAKE THE BEAT'S SPECIFICATION A JUDGED INPUT, NOT A CAPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM     must_be_perceptible and would_be_a_lie are precise, checkable requirements
            that no stage reads. This is the root cause, stated as a change.
WHY REAL    It is the only signal we have that distinguishes two beats sharing a job,
            and distinguishing them is the whole objective. Without it the ceiling on
            precision is set by how coarse the 20 jobs are, and narrate_an_event
            binding 133 records says that ceiling is very low.
CHANGE      A second judgment stage, per beat, over the already-narrowed candidate set
            from S1. Prompt file PROMPT-fit.md. Input: the beat's takeaway,
            must_be_perceptible, would_be_a_lie, entity_count and kind, plus each
            candidate's written description. Output per candidate: serves / conflicts /
            unclear, with cited evidence, carrying full provenance like every binding.
            Classification with evidence, never a score - per the design principle.
TEST        - a held-out set of beats the user labels once by hand; the stage must not
              return a candidate the user marked as conflicting
            - for beat 09 ("four percentages distinguishable as escalating values"), no
              returned candidate is one that cannot show four distinct values
            - every returned judgment carries promptSha, model, runId, evidence, or
              validate refuses it
COST        Judging one beat against ~20 candidates is roughly 3k in / 1k out, about
            $0.024 at Sonnet 5 rates. 500 beats/day is ~$12/day. The original premise
            that per-beat judgment was unaffordable does not survive contact with the
            actual numbers - the whole 425-record binding run cost $3.74.
            THIS IS A SPEND DECISION AND IS THE USER'S ALONE.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S3  MAKE THE BINDING KEY RICHER INSTEAD OF ADDING A STAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM     Same as S2.
WHY REAL    Same as S2.
CHANGE      Bind to (job, capacity band) rather than job alone, so
            narrate_an_event/one and narrate_an_event/several are different keys with
            different bound sets. Deterministic, no per-beat model call, no new spend.
TEST        - beats sharing a job but differing in band get different slates
            - 40 beats produce more than 20 distinct slates
TRADE       Cheaper than S2 and strictly weaker: it captures capacity and nothing else,
            so it cannot act on must_be_perceptible or would_be_a_lie. It is close to
            S1 folded into the key rather than applied as a filter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S4  FIX THE LIBRARY RATHER THAN THE SELECTOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM     45 records have no render. 49% declare no capacity. ~43 of 468 can carry a
            quantity at all.
WHY REAL    Selection cannot return a treatment that does not exist or that nobody can
            evaluate. If the library genuinely lacks the treatments the narration
            needs, every improvement to the selector is polish on a constraint that
            binds elsewhere.
CHANGE      Render the 45. Declare capacity on the records missing it. Prune designs
            that are poor, which only the user can see.
TEST        - every record in the pool has a clip
            - the share of records declaring capacity rises above a stated threshold
            - preflight fails if either regresses
NOTE        This competes with S1-S3 for the user's time, and may outrank them. It is
            listed because the analysis does not get to assume the selector is the
            binding constraint.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S5  MAKE THE EXISTING RULES REAL (correction, not a feature)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM     P4. FAM_MAX unread, CLUSTER_MAX inert, both tests vacuous.
WHY REAL    A rule the code does not enforce is a rule the user believes is protecting
            them and is not.
CHANGE      Read FAM_MAX in the loop. Make each cap test fail when its cap is removed.
            Remove clustering if, after S1-S3, it still moves only 12 rows.
TEST        - setting FAM_MAX=2 produces slates with two scenes from some family
        	- setting CLUSTER_MAX=1 changes at least one slate
            - both fail on the current code
STATUS      Logged as no-drifting entry 0010, open.

================================================================================
9. RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================
Do S5 first - it is a correction to work already claimed done, and it is small.
Then S1, because it is free, already built, and its absence is indefensible.

S2 versus S3 is the real architectural decision, and it is the user's: S3 keeps the
system free and deterministic but can only ever act on capacity; S2 acts on everything
the beat states, costs roughly $12/day at target scale, and puts a model back in the
per-beat path that the job-grammar inversion was specifically designed to remove.

I am not recommending between them in this document, because the first-principles
derivation requested for the next turn should reach its own answer without being
anchored to this one. Comparing the two independently is the point of doing both.

================================================================================
10. WHAT THIS ANALYSIS DOES NOT CLAIM
================================================================================
- It does not claim the job grammar was the wrong inversion. The grammar fixed a real
  failure: tags were non-discriminating, 4 tags sat on 80% of records, and ranking fell
  through to alphabetical. That diagnosis still holds.
- It does not claim the architecture must be redesigned. It identifies one specific,
  locatable flaw - the beat is compressed to a label before selection - which is
  addressable without a rewrite. Whether a rewrite is nonetheless the right call is
  exactly what the first-principles pass should test independently.
- It does not claim the defect list is complete. It is the list of defects that have
  been found, and the absence of tests until today means the found set is a sample.
