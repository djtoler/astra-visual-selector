## The shape of the problem

Five of twenty jobs are non-data and roughly four hundred records serve them. Fifteen are
data jobs and about seventy-three records serve them, of which six jobs have a hole and one
of those is the spine of the argument.

Supply is inverted against demand. Two thirds of beats want a data treatment and under a
sixth of the library can carry a number.

## Build spec — six treatments to author

These are not sourcing problems. The infographic system authors profiles and renders them,
so each hole is a spec.

1. **`one_vs_aggregate`** — one hero magnitude against one summed group magnitude, with the
   contributors identified but not separately ranked. Must not read as a pairwise matchup.
   Used four times in one video.
2. **`category_breakdown`** — labelled category counts summing to a stated total, no portrait
   slot, no ranking order, no time order. Text labels carry identity.
3. **`intersection_of_sets`** — two independently labelled threshold groups and the members
   clearing both. Must keep the two criteria distinct and never add them.
4. **`change_across_set`** — before and after position for a set of about ten, showing places
   moved, with one member that does not move.
5. **`explain_the_encoding`** — a chart that builds its own reading: axis meaning, one dot,
   then the field. Happens once per video and no template does it.
6. **`equivalence_restatement`** — one entity's total expressed in another entity's time
   units, with both bases visible and no false common axis.

`members_then_total` stays compound: an existing member sequence into an existing counter.
It needs a composed-treatment check, not a new profile.

## Rotation

Within one video, a treatment appears at most twice and never for adjacent beats. Across a
week in one niche, spread usage across a job's bound set before repeating. Jobs with a single
bound treatment cannot rotate, which is a second reason the holes matter.
