# Rotation policy — how to decide it

## The trap

The obvious policy is "vary treatments so videos do not look repetitive." I wrote that into
grammar v1: a treatment appears at most twice per video, never on adjacent beats.

That is wrong, and it contradicts the finding that started this whole direction. Harris,
Magnates and Voltgeist repeat their visual vocabulary relentlessly. The same map push, the
same archival photo move, for years. **The repetition is the brand.**

So the question is not how much to repeat. It is what to repeat and what to vary.

## The distinction that decides it

- Same treatment for the **same job** = grammar. The viewer learns that bars mean magnitude
  here, and every later beat reads faster. Repetition is doing work.
- Same treatment for **different jobs** = laziness. Two unrelated ideas wearing one picture.
- Different treatments for the **same job inside one video** = confusion. It teaches the
  viewer that the visual language means nothing.
- Different treatments for the same job **across videos** = freshness, but it erodes identity
  if it moves faster than a viewer can learn it.

## The policy that follows

**Inside one video: do not rotate.** One job, one treatment, locked for the whole runtime.
One-against-aggregate appears four times in Year Seventeen. All four should be the same
picture with different numbers. That is a running device, not monotony.

What varies inside a video is the mix of jobs, not the treatment per job. A twenty-five beat
video touching twelve distinct jobs already carries twelve distinct looks without rotating
anything.

**Across videos: the primary is fixed per niche.** Each job has one primary treatment for the
niche. Every video uses it. That consistency is what makes a channel recognisable.

**Alternates exist for data, not for variety.** Swap to an alternate only when:
- the arity falls outside the primary's tolerance, or
- the primary is contradicted by this beat's prohibitions, or
- the primary's asset does not exist for this subject.

**Refresh is deliberate and scheduled.** Change a job's primary on your clock, per series or
per season, never per video and never randomly. A viewer seeing three or four of your videos
a week needs the language to hold still long enough to learn it.

## Do not confuse the slate with the output

The review slate should be diverse, because you are choosing. The published output should be
consistent, because the viewer is learning. Your note that four near-identical portrait cards
were redundant was about the slate. It is not an argument for varying what ships.

## Parameters you set

1. **Primary per job, for this niche.** Twenty decisions. Most are already implied by the
   bindings you confirmed.
2. **Refresh cadence.** Per series, per quarter, or never until something better arrives.
3. **Alternate ceiling.** How many times a video may fall back to an alternate before the
   grammar is treated as failing for that script.

## Jobs that cannot rotate

Some jobs have one bound treatment. Under this policy that is not a defect. It is the primary
and there is nothing to decide.
